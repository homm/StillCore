StillCore

Understand where your Mac’s power goes. Keep an eye on activity, find demanding processes, and track battery drain — right from your menu bar.

Download v2.0.0 View source

Apple Silicon · macOS 14+ · Free & open source

Powerful hardware. A polished OS. What’s missing is a sense of control.
Why is your Mac warm? What’s draining the battery? Activity Monitor feels cumbersome when you just want a quick answer: by the time you open it and find your bearings, the spike may be gone. StillCore stays ready in your menu bar, so you can see what’s happening and decide what to do, whenever you need to.

Understand the activity

See what’s behind the spike.

Compare total system power with chip power, then see the CPU, GPU, and Neural Engine contributions. Move the chart marker over a spike to inspect the readings.

Frequency, usage, and temperature charts give you the context to understand what your Mac is doing.

From observation to action

Find the process. Choose what happens next.

See which processes are using the most CPU, GPU, memory, or CPU power. Filter by application, process name, or PID to narrow the list.

Once you’ve found a demanding process, you can quit it or apply a background policy directly from StillCore.

Away from the charger

Make sense of every unplugged session.

See how much charge remains and how much you’ve used since unplugging, with separate totals for the time your Mac spent awake and asleep.

Switch between Automatic and Power Save modes for your current power source as your priorities change.

There when you need it

A small place in your menu bar.

Choose the metrics you want at a glance, along with a battery icon or percentage. Open the charts when something catches your attention, or detach the window to keep them visible while you work.

Set the update interval from 100 ms to 10 seconds, whether you’re watching a brief burst of activity or keeping an eye on everyday use.

Low overhead by design

Built to stay on.

A monitor is most useful when it’s already running. StillCore is built for continuous, everyday monitoring with low overhead, so it’s always there when something suddenly feels off.
